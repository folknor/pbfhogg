## orchestration

SCOPE: orchestration, processes and the reference cache. Files: `crates/verify/src/main.rs`, `all.rs`, `run.rs`, `selection.rs`, `harness.rs`, `process.rs`, `stream.rs`, `reference.rs`, `reference_cache.rs`, `tunables.rs`, `argv.rs`, `output.rs`. The cost to optimize is the wall time of the whole `verify all` run on denmark, paid every time a developer verifies: how many pbfhogg and osmium processes run, how often the same input is read, decoded, sorted or converted again across checks, what the reference cache does and does not reuse within and across runs, what runs serially that could overlap, and how much scratch I/O is written and re-read. The pbfhogg and osmium binaries' own speed is out of scope; what the verifier makes them do, how many times, is in scope. Each check's comparison logic belongs to sibling scopes (the shared decode/compare engine, and the checks themselves); your seam is how checks are arranged, what they share, and how reference work is produced. Propose what the checks should share and how they should be scheduled, and flag what the check side would have to change to allow it.

## engine

SCOPE: the shared decode and compare engine. Files: `crates/verify/src/opl_compare.rs`, `verify_opl.rs`, `verify_diff_opl.rs`, `verify_diff_detail.rs`, `verify_element_diff.rs`, `verify_change_listing.rs`, `pbf_frames.rs`, `osc.rs`, `diff_summary.rs`, `verify_reencode.rs`, `verify_input_order.rs`, `verify_extsort.rs`, `counts.rs`. The cost to optimize is paid once per check per output, many times in a `verify all`: turning PBFs and OSCs into comparable form (OPL text, frames, models), materializing and sorting it, and comparing it. Whether OPL text is the right intermediate at all is open; so is whether the verifier should read PBF itself through the pbfhogg library or another reader instead of through osmium cat. Run orchestration and caching is the orchestration scope's; check-specific models are the check scopes'. Propose the representation the checks and orchestration should want from you, and flag what they would need to change to use it.

## heavy-checks

SCOPE: the heavy checks. Files: `crates/verify/src/checks/degrade.rs`, `add_locations.rs`, `apply_changes.rs`, `apply_model.rs`, `merge_changes.rs`, `field_policies.rs`, `sort.rs`, `renumber.rs`, `cat_dedupe.rs`, `dedupe_plan.rs`, `cat.rs`, `repack.rs`. Observed walls on denmark: degrade 4 to 9 minutes, add-locations-to-ways 2.6 to 6.7, apply-changes up to 4.6, sort 2.6. The cost to optimize is each check's own wall: the pbfhogg and reference runs it commissions (how many variants, how many passes over the same input), the verifier-side models it builds, and how it walks the data. The shared decode/compare engine is a sibling scope's and run-level sequencing and caching is orchestration's; where your check's cost is really theirs, say what you need from them. Be concrete about which variants or sub-runs inside a check are redundant with each other or with another check.
